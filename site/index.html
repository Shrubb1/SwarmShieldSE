What it catches — in plain English

The detection classes, translated to what they actually mean for you.

injection

Code sneaked into your requests

SQLi, XSS, command injection, template attacks — on sight, even disguised (leet-speak, encoded, comment-spliced).

behavior

Attacks that look different every time

The SNN learns your normal — floods, beacons, coordinated scans across many IPs get flagged by shape, not signature.

prediction

Attacks stopped before they land

Kill-chain tracking spots recon → enumeration patterns and blocks the exploit before it ever reaches you.

evasion

Tricks that beat real firewalls

Request smuggling, charset confusion, JSON pollution, slow-drip attacks — the classes that slip past commercial WAFs.

network

Anything phoning home

DNS exfil dies at the lookup. Credential/API-key material can't leave the machine. Beacons get tarpitted.

identity

Attackers who switch IPs

Fingerprinting recognizes the same actor on a new address — rotation is detected on the first event.

It gets stronger every day

Every bypass ever found becomes a permanent regression — the corpus can never re-open. Learned fixes share anonymously across paid clients (herd immunity); your learned state survives restarts via checkpoints.
